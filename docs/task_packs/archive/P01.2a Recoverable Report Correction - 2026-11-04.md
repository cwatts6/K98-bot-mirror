## Scope and architecture

Report 72 confirms deployed startup attribution but exposes an ordinary startup
summary that exceeds its independent byte limit. Review also identified dropped
coverage reasons, cache coverage inconsistent with delivered content, optional
monitor detail preventing a fitting incident, discarded collection progress and
a diagnostic token consumed before an uncertain Discord delivery.

These are P01.2a collection/consumer acceptance obligations. Services retain
projection, authorization and collection ownership; the view only renders counts
and carries the supplied continuation cursor. No SQL/DAL, provider protocol,
business retry, source producer, persistence reader/writer or mandatory receipt
change is intended. Existing serializers, redaction, frozen component storage,
owned offload and physical report admission are reused.

Ordinary and ZIP startup consumers share one projection. Each valid bounded row
survives independently, with available/included/omitted counts. Ordinary reports
retain legacy source-status fields and add component coverage with reasons and
capture times. Delivery-budget omission changes delivered coverage; it does not
claim the underlying provider failed. Invalid operation-preview rows do not hide
valid neighboring rows. Optional monitor and operation data yield to a fitting
incident, and the final encoded attachment is checked.

Incident collection retains an exact, contiguous JSONL prefix. A trial compressor
checks each bounded page and falls back to ordered rows at a byte boundary; count,
hash and cursor advance together.
The result includes a valid ZIP, honest partial summary and the last included
incident. Existing incident pagination continues from that cursor and cutoff.
The cutoff fixes first-seen membership, not incident state: collection remains
explicitly nontransactional. Failed reads never advance the cursor. A native read
that returns after the deadline retains its bounded page before another read is
considered. Cancellation/shutdown still prevent delivery and retain physical
ownership until settlement. Read/projection timings explain collection cost
without attributing SQL lock holders or provider outcomes.

Diagnostic continuation advances once and retains the exact latest produced part
for authorized retry after failed/uncertain delivery. Retries use identical bytes,
hash, generation and successor; they do not recollect or extend expiry. Producing
the next part replaces the previous retry slot. A changed actor, guild, channel,
scope or expired capability cannot retrieve it. Initial delivery failure exposes
no advanced incident cursor; another initial request is a new observation.

## Concrete bounds and compatibility

- Cooperative collection budget: 30 seconds, independent of business-operation
  duration and physical worker settlement. One report build/delivery owns the slot.
- Incident staging: at most 32 MiB uncompressed; compressed prefix reserves 32 KiB
  of attachment space for metadata and diagnostics/continuation. Attachment limits
  remain 64 KiB minimum and 8 MiB maximum.
- Startup inventory: at most 64 rows. Ordinary startup projection: 8 KiB compact
  JSON; complete component: 128 KiB. Omitted/invalid rows are counted.
- Ordinary monitor aggregate: 24 KiB content allocation plus coverage reserve,
  qualified against a 32 KiB final pretty-JSON bound. Individual providers retain
  their existing schema/depth limits; this does not permit arbitrary objects.
- Diagnostic retention: at most two generations, each with at most 17 MiB pending
  components and one retry artifact bounded by 8 MiB. Ten-minute expiry is never
  refreshed by retries. Drain clears pending components and retry artifacts.
- Download sessions are disposable diagnostics, not durable business state.
  Restart/expiry requires a new explicitly identified collection. Already saved
  artifacts remain usable; collection never authorizes business replay.
- Existing report field names and schemas remain; coverage/cursor/timing fields
  are additive. Historical V1/V2 producer/persistence contracts remain unchanged.
  All 60 installed updater transitions remain immutable; this source family adds
  an exact successor transition requiring the reviewed updater registry.
